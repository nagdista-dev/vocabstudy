VocabStudy guide · Arabic-first

The English words you meet, made to stay.

VocabStudy is a free, open-source app for Arabic speakers. Add the word you met in a series, an article or an email, write a sentence with it, and recall it on spaced days. No account, no ads, and it works offline.

Last updated: 28 November 20266 min readBy Nagdista

The short answer

VocabStudy is a free web app built for Arabic speakers learning English. You add a word you actually met, and it brings the Arabic meaning, an English definition, a real example and pronunciation. Then it brings the word back in short recall tests after 1, 3, 7, 14, 30 and 60 days. No account, no ads, and it works offline after the first visit.

What VocabStudy is

Most vocabulary apps start with a ready-made list: 1,000 words, or a course from A1 to C1. VocabStudy starts empty. The idea is that a word you met yourself, in a series, an article or a work email, already has a context in your memory. That context is the strongest cue you have.

Type the word, paste a paragraph and pick the words you do not know, or import a list from Excel or Google Sheets. The app brings the Arabic meaning, the English definition, an example and the pronunciation. You write a sentence from your own life. Then the reviews begin.

The interface is in English on purpose: every button and sentence is a small English exercise. Arabic appears where it teaches you something: the meaning of the word, and the mistakes Arabic speakers make with it.

Who it is for

Three kinds of learners. Not someone who wants a complete course from zero.

School and university students
You want to fix the vocabulary from your textbook and your reading, and you need the Arabic meaning before the English one. Add the words from each lesson and review them before the exam.
Professionals who use English at work
You read emails, reports and meeting notes. You collect the words that slow you down, learn them in context, and keep your data on your own device.
Self-learners and IELTS candidates
You build your own vocabulary and want a review system that stops the words from fading. Import your lists and tag them IELTS, Work or Travel.

How it works

Four steps, then the review becomes a short daily habit.

  1. 1

    Add a word from anywhere

    Type it in the app, or paste a paragraph and pick the words you do not know. On a phone you can share a word from the browser; on a computer you can use the bookmarks-bar button. If you add it from a paragraph, the sentence stays with it.

  2. 2

    Learn it once, properly

    You get the Arabic meaning, an English definition, a real example and a recording in American or British English. You see the stressed syllable, and whether the word is one of the 1,000 most common words or a rare one, based on a 30,000-word frequency list.

  3. 3

    Write a sentence from your life

    In Use it, you write a sentence with the word. The app checks it on your device: is the word there, is it a full sentence, did you copy the example, and did you fall into a common Arabic-speaker trap.

  4. 4

    Recall it on spaced days

    It does not ask “do you know it?”. It asks you to produce the word from the meaning or from a sentence with a gap. Each successful recall pushes the next review further away. If you only have five minutes, the 5-minute review gives you the ten words that matter most.

Review schedule After the first lesson, the word returns after 1 day, then 3, 7, 14, 30 and 60. The aim is to recall it just before you would forget it, not after.

What makes it different

Six small decisions that change the learning experience.

Arabic first, not machine translation
The Arabic meaning comes with 112 hand-written usage traps: depend of on, the difference between borrow and lend, actually not meaning “currently”, and advice never taking an an.
Active recall, not a false sense of knowing
Reading a word and its meaning feels comfortable, but it does not make the word stick. The app makes you produce the word yourself, which is the exercise that builds memory.
Your words stay on your device
No account, no ads, and no server that stores your words. Every word and every step is saved in your browser. You can export a complete backup as one file.
Free and open source
The code is published under the MIT licence. No subscription, no paid tier, no feature hidden behind a paywall.
Works offline
After the first visit, the whole app works without a connection. You can install it on your home screen like any other app.
Know whether a word is worth your time
Every word shows how common it is, from Top 1,000 to Rare, so you start with the words that help most in reading, exams and work.

What you will not find

These are deliberate limits, not hidden flaws.
  • No ready-made word lists or graded course from A1 to C1. The library starts empty, and you choose the words.
  • The interface is in English only. Arabic lives in the meanings and the usage traps, but the buttons and sentences are English.
  • No automatic sync between devices. Moving to another device is manual, through a QR code or a file.
  • It is an installable web app (PWA), not a native app in the App Store or Google Play.
  • It focuses on vocabulary. It does not teach conversation or grammar from scratch, though it supports both.

If you want a complete course that starts from the alphabet, this is not a replacement. Use VocabStudy alongside a course, a book or a teacher, to make what you learn there stick.

Start in five minutes

You do not need a big plan. Five words from your day are enough.

  1. Open the app in your browser.
  2. Add five words you met today: from a series, an article, an email or a lecture.
  3. Write one sentence for each word, about something that actually happened to you.
  4. Come back tomorrow and press Start recall. The words will be waiting.
  5. Install the app on your home screen, and export a backup once a week.

If you cannot find five words, start with one. The word you met today and did not save is the one you will forget tomorrow.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes, all of it, permanently. No subscription, no ads, no paid feature. The code is published under the MIT licence, so anyone can read it or contribute.

Does it work offline?

Yes, after the first visit. A few optional features need a connection: fetching a new pronunciation recording, extra examples from Tatoeba, or grammar checking through LanguageTool. You can turn those services off in Settings.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no sign-up and no email address. Your words and progress are stored in your browser on your device.

Are there ready-made word lists?

No, by design. The best word to learn is the one you met and needed. If you already have a list in Excel or Google Sheets, you can paste or import it once.

Is it suitable for beginners?

It is built for learners from about A2 to C1. The interface is English but short and simple, and Arabic is there for the meaning and the traps. If you are at A1, start with very few words and keep a dictionary nearby.

What if I clear my browser data?

You will lose your words if you have no backup. The app reminds you once a week to export one, and you can restore everything from a single file.

How is it different from Anki?

Anki is more powerful and more flexible, but it needs setup and content you make or find yourself. VocabStudy is simpler, closer to an Arabic speaker’s needs, and comes with ready explanations of Arabic-speaker traps. Read the full comparison.
