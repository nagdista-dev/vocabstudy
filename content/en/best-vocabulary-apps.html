Best vocabulary apps for Arabic speakers: a 2026 comparison

Six well-known apps, judged on five things that matter to an Arabic speaker. There is no single winner: the right choice depends on whether you want your own words, a ready-made course, or a powerful review system.

Last updated: 28 November 20269 min readBy Nagdista

The short answer

If you want to keep the words you meet, with Arabic explanations and Arabic-speaker traps, start with VocabStudy. If you want the most powerful spaced-repetition system and full control, choose Anki. If you want a ready-made course with native-speaker videos, Memrise. If you study with a class or shared sets, Quizlet. Duolingo is best for building a daily habit, and LanGeek for vocabulary organized by CEFR level.

A note on “Arabic-first”

It does not mean the app is merely translated into Arabic. It means the explanations, meanings and examples were written for someone whose first language is Arabic. Anki, for example, can display its interface in Arabic, but its content and templates are mostly English, and you write or find the Arabic meaning yourself.

The apps, one by one

What each app does well, and what you may not like.

VocabStudy Free and open source

An Arabic-first web app for the words you actually meet. Type a word or paste a paragraph, and it brings the Arabic meaning, an English definition, an example and pronunciation. Then it reviews the word on spaced days. It works offline after the first visit and needs no account.

  • Strength: ready Arabic explanations, 112 Arabic-speaker usage traps, and active recall by typing the word.
  • Limit: no ready-made study decks or course lists, an English interface, and no automatic sync between devices.

Choose it if: you want a personal word notebook with Arabic support and you care about privacy.

Anki The strongest review system

A flexible flashcard program for desktop and mobile, with advanced spaced-repetition algorithms. It has a huge library of shared decks and add-ons that let you customize almost anything. Free on desktop and Android; a one-time purchase on iPhone.

  • Strength: full control, a large community, and ready decks for exams such as IELTS.
  • Limit: a long setup, a steep learning curve, and Arabic content is mostly your responsibility.

Choose it if: you like manual control, want a system that lasts for years, and do not mind an hour of setup.

Memrise Courses and videos

A language-learning platform built on ready-made lessons and videos of native speakers. It has a general English course and short daily exercises, and Arabic support varies by course and country. The free tier is limited; full access needs a subscription.

  • Strength: a fast start, ready content, and pronunciation from real people.
  • Limit: less control over your own words, and you follow the platform’s content rather than the words you meet.

Choose it if: you want to start today with an organized course and do not want to build your own list.

Quizlet Flashcards and class sets

A flashcard platform with millions of sets made by students and teachers. It supports tests, writing and matching. It is built for studying together and for class sets, with a free tier and a paid plan.

  • Strength: a huge library and easy sharing with classmates.
  • Limit: content quality varies, there are no Arabic-speaker traps, and spaced review is not the core of the product.

Choose it if: you already have a set from your class or school and want a quick review with friends.

Duolingo The daily habit

A gamified language app that builds a daily habit from short lessons. It has an English course for Arabic speakers. The free version shows ads; the paid version removes them and adds features.

  • Strength: excellent motivation, an easy start, and good coverage of basic grammar.
  • Limit: it is not a word notebook, you do not control the vocabulary, and the review follows the course rather than your words.

Choose it if: your problem is consistency, and you want an app that makes you open it every day.

LanGeek Vocabulary by level

An English-learning platform with Arabic localization, vocabulary organized by CEFR level, grammar, reading and expressions. It offers ready lists and exercises, with a free tier and a paid plan.

  • Strength: clear level organization and better Arabic support than many global platforms.
  • Limit: closer to a structured course than a personal word notebook, and full access needs a subscription.

Choose it if: you want a path from A1 to C2 and prefer Arabic explanations.

How we chose

We did not compare word counts alone. A large list is useless if the words are not the ones you need. We used five criteria:

  1. Arabic support: are the meaning and explanation written for an Arabic speaker, or machine-translated?
  2. Context: does the app show the word in a real sentence, or only as a word and a translation?
  3. Review: is there active recall and spaced repetition, or only reading and repetition?
  4. Ownership: can you keep the words you meet, or must you follow the platform’s list?
  5. Cost and privacy: does it work offline, does it need an account, and is the price fair?

Questions

What is the best vocabulary app for Arabic speakers?

There is no single answer. If your priority is Arabic explanations and your own words, VocabStudy fits. If your priority is a powerful review system and full control, Anki. If you want a ready course, Memrise or LanGeek. What matters most is that the app is part of a habit of reading and listening, not a replacement for it.

Is Anki better than VocabStudy?

Anki is more powerful and flexible, but it asks for setup time and content you make or find yourself. VocabStudy is simpler and more Arabic-first, but less flexible. There is no absolute “better”, only a better fit for how you learn.

Are the free versions enough?

Usually yes at the start. VocabStudy is completely free, and Anki is free on desktop and Android. The paid plans in Memrise, Quizlet, Duolingo and LanGeek add features, but they are not required to learn. Try the free version for a month before paying.

Does Memrise support Arabic?

Memrise offers English courses and its interface is available in several languages, but Arabic support varies by course and country. The content still belongs to the platform, not to the words you meet yourself. Check the official site for what is available in your country right now.

Can I use more than one app?

Yes, but do not spread the same words across three apps. Pick one app as your main notebook, and use the others for listening, grammar or speaking. Scattered attention is a bigger risk than a missing app.

Are the prices in the table accurate?

The table gives a general picture as of November 2026, because prices vary by country and change with promotions. Check each app’s official pricing page before you subscribe.
